# Project summary — current prototype draft

SinkingCities explores how satellite radar can make coastal land subsidence understandable across Southeast Asia. When land sinks, communities can face greater relative sea-level rise even before accounting for changes in the ocean.

Our current prototype uses real NASA–ISRO NISAR catalog data to map observation coverage across five coastal cities, inspect acquisition dates and identify continuous interferogram sequences. It makes a crucial distinction visible: a satellite observation is not yet a trustworthy ground-motion measurement. Semarang provides our first candidate sequence for detailed processing.

The next stage will turn quality-controlled radar phase into referenced displacement time series, with coherence and uncertainty shown alongside results. A later scenario can combine validated land motion with NASA sea-level projections. Our aim is an accessible evidence tool for researchers, educators and local planners—not an unvalidated flood forecast.

Status disclosure: surface-change measurements and 2050 scenarios are not yet implemented. Update this draft only after real results are produced and checked.
